# The core idea behind SFDC24.com

**Stated by Mr. Salam, 4 September 2026. This is the thesis every instance
builds toward. If a decision does not serve it, it is the wrong decision.**

---

## The one sentence

**The sale happens when the visitor already has a working thing in their hands
— not when they are reading a quote.**

---

## Why that is the whole strategy

A consultancy normally sells a promise. The buyer receives a proposal, a rate, a
timeline, and a set of references, and is then asked to imagine the outcome.
That puts them straight into the mode where every competitor is a spreadsheet
row: comparing quotes, weighing vendors, thinking about procurement — the
typical jazz. It is a mode where nobody is excited and the smallest price wins.

SFDC24 does not compete there. **It hands the buyer the thing.** Once someone is
holding a working version of their own idea, the conversation stops being
"which vendor" and becomes "how do I keep this". That is a different, better
question, and it is one only we can answer, because we already built it.

---

## What the visitor actually experiences

Four beats. They must happen in this order, and the first three must be fast.

**1. They talk. It gets built in front of them.**
While they are describing the problem, they watch their idea assemble itself on
screen. Simplest possible form — **a Lego build, not a cathedral.** Blocky,
obvious, recognisably theirs. The point is not fidelity; the point is that the
thing in their head is now outside their head, and they can point at it.

**2. We check we heard them right.**
A short, specific validation: *is this what you meant?* Not a discovery
questionnaire, not a requirements document. **Do not go deep here.** Going deep
is how this turns back into consulting, and consulting is what we are replacing.
One round of "yes, but the X should be Y" is the target.

**3. We build it.**
The real version, from the prototype they already approved. They have seen where
it is going, so this is confirmation, not suspense.

**4. Then, and only then, we ask them to pay to make it their own.**
Money is raised against something they can already use. The ask lands as
"keep this" rather than "fund this". A buyer who has used the thing has no
reason to shop it around, because a competitor cannot show them this — they can
only describe it.

---

## What follows from this, concretely

- **The prototype IS the pitch.** Effort spent on marketing copy that describes
  what we could do is effort spent in the wrong mode. Show, do not tell.
- **Speed beats polish in beats 1 and 2.** A rough thing they can see in ninety
  seconds beats a beautiful thing in a week. Blocky is a feature; it signals
  "this is a sketch, tell me what is wrong with it" and invites correction.
- **The page must remodel itself around the visitor** — what they are trying to
  do and what state they are in. A static brochure cannot do beat 1. This is why
  the site had to leave Google Sites, and why the assistant is the product
  rather than a widget bolted onto a brochure.
- **Talking must never cost them sight of the detail.** Voice is an input, not a
  mode that hides the screen. While they talk, the build stays visible — they
  still need to see enough to make a decision.
- **Never ask for money before there is something working.** The order is not
  negotiable. Reversing it puts us back in the quote-comparison pile.
- **Never make the visitor do the work of being understood.** They are busy.
  They will not fill in forms, write specifications or file bug reports. If
  something is broken, the site notices and reports itself.

---

## What this is not

- Not a demo library. A canned example is somebody else's idea; the whole effect
  depends on it being *theirs*.
- Not a requirements-gathering funnel with a prototype at the end. The prototype
  comes first, in the same conversation.
- Not free custom development. Beats 1 and 2 are cheap by design — simplest
  form, one validation round. Beat 4 is where the work is paid for.

## For other instances reading this

Cite this file by name in board rows rather than restating it. If a piece of

back into ordinary consultancy-website behaviour: more copy, more pages, more
