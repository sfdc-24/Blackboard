Write one missing section of a consultancy homepage. Output only the section
copy, ready to paste. No preamble, no explanation, no options to choose from.

THE PROBLEM WITH THE PAGE TODAY
It has four sections, each naming a Salesforce problem the reader recognises:
automation nobody dares touch, reporting nobody trusts, process and system
drifted apart, and nobody owning the next decision. They are well written and
the reader agrees with all four. Then the page asks them to email.

Nowhere does it say what they would actually RECEIVE. A reader can nod four
times and still have no idea what buying looks like. That is the gap.

WHAT TO WRITE
A section that makes the first step concrete: a fixed-scope diagnostic that ends
in a written recommendation. Cover, in the reader's terms:
- what happens (what gets looked at, roughly how long it takes)
- what lands on their desk at the end, as an object they can picture
- what it commits them to afterwards, which is nothing

HARD CONSTRAINTS
- NO PRICE. The pricing decision is not made. Do not imply free, do not imply
  expensive, do not say "starting at". Write it so a price can be added later
  in one sentence without rewriting anything.
- Invent nothing. There are no clients, no case studies, no named results, no
  team, no certifications, no tools you have not been told about. One
  independent consultant in Toronto whose background is Salesforce operations,
  Lean Six Sigma and Scrum. AI agents do the legwork; he signs off on anything
  consequential.
- No timeline promise you cannot keep. "A week or two" is fine if hedged;
  "within 48 hours" is not.
- Do not describe the AI. Another section covers it. This one is about what the
  buyer gets.

VOICE
Direct, specific, no marketing adjectives. Never the words transformation,
innovation, seamless, empower, unlock, or AI-powered. Plain sentences. If a
line could sit on any consultancy's page, cut it. Around 150-220 words. Give it
a heading. Assume the reader is a busy operations lead who has been burned by a
vendor before.
